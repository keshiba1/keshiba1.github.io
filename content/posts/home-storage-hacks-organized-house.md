A house looks cluttered not because it is small, but because small things have no fixed place — give every item one dedicated spot and even a big home instantly looks clean and organized.

Things accumulate in our homes without our wanting them to, and once small items stay scattered across surfaces, we lose the space to keep them and the whole house starts to feel untidy. The fix is not more cleaning — it is assigning every object a home.

## Where should I keep tiny daily items like safety pins and clips?

The items that vanish fastest are the smallest ones: safety pins, clips and rubber bands. You always need them — especially when you are getting ready, wearing a *saree*, and cannot find a safety pin anywhere. Keep them together in one small box near your dressing table.

Into that one box goes everything of that kind:

1. Safety pins in different sizes, for different dresses.
2. Rubber bands and a few magnets that come in handy.
3. Clips and different types of hair pins.
4. A separate small box for spare clips, since they break quickly and you always keep extras.
5. A separate box or case for the hair dryer, so its wires do not get cut and dust stays out.
6. A small box for bangles and bracelets you wear only sometimes, so they do not spoil.

Use a transparent box wherever you can — you will see everything at a glance and never rummage before a function. Wipe the boxes on top during regular dusting so dust does not settle inside, and even with many things stored together, your dressing area stays clean instead of cluttered.

## How do I add storage without drilling nails into the wall?

It is not always possible to hammer nails at home, and some walls simply will not hold them. For holders and small shelves, use adhesive stickers instead — they are easy to apply, easily available on Amazon, and quite strong.

This makes it simple to repurpose what you already own. A spare kitchen shelf, for example, can be moved to the bathroom to hold three or four items that were otherwise lying on the window sill. Without any real effort, an organizer like this keeps small things in order and makes the bathroom look clean.

## Can I get storage without side tables taking up floor space?

We rely on side tables for glasses, the phone and chargers, but they eat up floor space. Hidden storage solves this. A bed built with pull-out storage on both sides works exactly like drawers, so your outside space is never wasted and the room looks more spacious.

Whenever you get a bed made, build storage into it — a lot fits inside. Storage set into the headrest, or small drawers along the sides, is perfect for the mobile, charger or books you reach for while sitting on the bed.

## How should I store seasonal clothes and expensive sarees?

Swapping warm clothes and daily-wear clothes every couple of months creates a huge amount of repeat work. Instead, dedicate one whole wardrobe to warm clothes and seasonal items you do not need often, segregated into baskets and bags. Good storage should not need rearranging again and again.

Costly *silk* sarees need a little more care, but only once:

1. Never hang sarees — hanging spoils them fast, and the hanger mark slowly starts to tear the fabric.
2. Store each saree flat in a saree bag.
3. Fold a sheet of paper into the middle of every saree; butter paper protects better than newspaper, especially for silk.
4. Fit six to seven sarees per bag — one or two extra for silk, four to five for cotton.
5. No saree bag? A cloth shopping bag works too, as long as paper still separates each saree.

This keeps the *zari* borders and silver-and-gold colours from dulling, so the saree stays exactly as it was when you bought it.

## Does the way I place an organizer really save space?

Yes — how you position an organizer decides how much storage you create. Stand a clothes organizer holding *kurtas* and suit sets vertically rather than laying it flat. Placed horizontally, the pieces stay pressed one under the other, so you lift each one to find a dress and the back space is wasted. Upright, you see every item in front of you, store the same number of clothes, and free up space ahead of it for more things.

## What is the easiest way to keep the house tidy every day?

When everything has a place, the house does not get dirty again and again — but small things still drift onto surfaces. Keep one big basket for exactly this. On your rounds of the house, especially on days you do not feel like cleaning, collect every stray item — pencils, erasers, books — into that basket, then return them to their spots when you have time.

A tray helps with plants too. If you grow money plant and other plants in water across the living room, kitchen and bedroom, the water needs changing every two days or it turns dirty and attracts insects. Gather all the plants onto one tray, carry them together, and bring them back the same way to save time and energy.

## How do I stop mops and brooms making the bathroom look messy?

Use an organizer made specially for mops, or strong hooks. Mops left lying around make the floor dirty and the bathroom look untidy. Attach mops to the organizer easily; for lighter mops, tie a small rope and hang them from the strong hooks. Brooms and dusting tools stay tidy the same way.

Give every small and big thing a fixed place, and your house will never look cluttered or feel untidy again.
