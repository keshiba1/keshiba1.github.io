Keeping your kitchen clean all day is less about deep-cleaning and more about tiny habits — put each thing back the moment you finish with it, and keep your countertop as empty as possible.

Most homemakers get tired of organising the kitchen again and again. The reason is rarely a lack of effort — it's that things pile up on the counter and around the sink faster than we can put them away. These habits are deliberately simple, because a kitchen system that adds fuss becomes a burden you eventually abandon.

## How do I stop my kitchen countertop from looking cluttered?

The counter is the first thing that turns messy, so protect it. When you buy a week's worth of vegetables, don't leave them sitting out — they spoil and make the counter look dirty. Small tools like scissors and knives that you use while cooking should go straight back to their place the moment you're done. An empty counter gives you room to work and instantly looks clean.

1. Put away vegetables as soon as you bring them in — don't let them sit on the counter.
2. Return knives, scissors, and small tools to their spot immediately after use.
3. Dry, fold, and store kitchen towels; never leave a used towel lying on the counter.
4. Keep the counter as empty as you can whenever possible.

Even a spotless counter looks untidy with one messy towel on it, so this small discipline pays off all day.

## What is the best way to organise vegetables in the fridge?

Use boxes. Dumping vegetables into the fridge "any which way" leaves them mixing together and spoiling. Boxes keep everything separated, visible, and easy to grab while cooking.

Keep the things you reach for in almost every dish — green chillies, coriander leaves, curry leaves — together in one box, since they go into *sabzi*, *dal*, and everything else. You can add peeled or chopped garlic to that box too, which stops the garlic smell from spreading through the fridge. This saves you from running back and forth for tiny things and forgetting one every time.

Choose boxes with a mesh tray at the bottom. When you wash vegetables before storing, some stay a little wet — the mesh lets that extra moisture drain down and settle below, so the vegetables dry out and stay fresh instead of rotting. Even slightly wet ginger dries out on its own in the fridge in a box like this. For vegetables you buy in bulk, like bitter gourd, use a larger box or split them across two.

## Can I organise my fridge without spending money?

Yes. If you don't want to invest in containers, reuse the plastic boxes that food parcels arrive in. Poke a few small holes in the bottom so any extra water from wet vegetables drains out before the box goes into the fridge. Line these boxes up in the vegetable tray and the fridge looks tidy, with everything fresh and visible — no more discovering a forgotten vegetable gone bad at the back.

## How do I keep the kitchen sink area clean?

The sink is busy all day, but that's no excuse to leave it messy. Store your dishwashing soap and scrubbers in a small transparent box — you don't need a large one. Five or six scrubbers are usually plenty, though a bulk pack works if you have space to store it tucked away. A soap dish that drains water keeps scrubbers from looking dirty on top.

Use separate brushes for cleaning the sink and for cleaning dishes. Throw out whatever collects in the drain mesh right away, rinse the mesh, and put it back. Whether you wash dishes twice or four times a day, once you're done, dry and wipe the sink and return everything to its place. Walk in next time to a clean, dry sink — it lifts your mood, makes work easier, and even makes a small sink area feel more spacious.

## How can I make cooking cleanup faster?

Spread out a newspaper before you chop vegetables or onions. This doesn't add work — it cuts it. Collect all the peels and scraps on the paper, and when you're finished cooking, fold it up and drop it in the dustbin. The surface underneath never gets dirty, so you skip a whole cleaning step.

## Should I prepare spice blends at home in advance?

Make your *garam masala* and other blends in one monthly batch instead of daily. Roast everything together, let it cool, then grind. Doing this once a month — or every 15 days — means your *masala* is always ready and you skip a time-consuming job on cooking days. Because it involves roasting, cooling, and checking your stock, it's best done on one dedicated day.

Home-made red chilli powder and coriander powder are worth the effort too — store-bought spices often carry adulteration, and fresh ones taste noticeably different. One rule: always label your bottles. Ground spices look almost identical, and labels save you from wasting time telling them apart.

## What baking tricks make cakes easier to handle?

Two small changes make a big difference:

1. **Grease the tin with oil, not butter or ghee.** Butter and ghee solidify as the cake cools, forcing you to reheat the tin to release the cake. Oil stays liquid, so the cake slides out easily even when fully cooled.
2. **Cut a perfect lining circle by folding.** Fold parchment or butter paper into a triangle cone, set the point at the centre of your round tin, mark the edge, and trim the excess. Unfolded, it's a neat circle sized exactly to your tin. Never use newspaper — its ink can transfer onto the cake.

All of these tips sound simple, and that's the point. Follow them consistently and your kitchen stays organised and clutter-free without any extra fuss.
