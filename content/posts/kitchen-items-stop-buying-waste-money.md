The smartest kitchen purchase you can make is to stop buying organizers that don't actually suit your space — because an organizer that works in someone else's home can be useless in yours.

Before you copy a setup you saw online or in a friend's kitchen, look at your own kitchen first. Measure the space, check which organizers will genuinely fit, and only then buy. Otherwise you are just wasting money on pretty things you can't use. Here is a clear-eyed rundown of the kitchen items worth skipping — and what to buy instead.

## Which storage racks should I avoid buying?

Some racks look so good that we buy them without thinking them through. A slim wheeled rack is genuinely useful for very tight spaces where you have no room at all — but the wheels stop working the moment you add even a little weight. Load it up and it shakes; put three or four items on it and the wheels won't move. It has to rest against a wall for support, or it can tip over at any time. Only get this if you have a wall to lean it against.

A magazine or newspaper holder has the same problem. Add a little weight on top and it tips backward, so keeping it stable is genuinely difficult. Use it only for newspapers or a few small things — pile on magazines or books and it falls over repeatedly.

Open shelving units carry a hidden cost too: they get dirty very fast, and a damp cloth alone won't clean them. You have to remove everything and take each shelf out separately to clean it properly. They suit light items like potatoes, onions, and tomatoes, but not anything heavy.

## Are jute baskets practical for the kitchen?

Jute baskets are a favourite for their looks — but they are absolutely not practical for kitchen storage. If you keep a bottle of oil in one and it leaks, no amount of washing removes the stain. Even careful use leaves residue at the base of a bottle, and the basket base stains permanently. Don't store *haldi* or spice boxes in them either.

Their best uses are away from the stove:

1. Small clothing items inside your wardrobe.
2. Jewellery boxes.
3. Sealed, packaged goods like soap and closed grocery packets.
4. Bathroom items — soaps, toothpaste, handwash, and other liquids, which look neat and stay clean.

## Are small decorative baskets worth it?

Tiny baskets are mostly useless because their shape won't let you fit anything meaningful inside. A small red-and-orange basket holds a few fruits or vegetables at most — but put vegetables in it and try to fit it in the fridge, and it eats up space while not matching the vegetable trays most fridges come with. Realistically you can only use it outside the fridge, for onions or potatoes. Don't waste money on baskets like this.

## Which rotating tray (lazy Susan) is worth buying?

Rotating trays are actually very useful — but quality decides everything. The cheap ₹99–150 tray is poor quality: the plastic is so soft that four bottles won't rotate properly, it wobbles, and it warps within a few days under kitchen weight.

A sturdier, better-quality rotating tray costs a bit more but holds heavy items and large containers, fits more of them, stays stable, and can safely carry glass bottles. When you invest in your kitchen, invest in good quality.

## Should I keep glass bottles in the kitchen?

Glass bottles are best avoided for everyday use. If one falls, you lose the food inside, create a mess, and scatter shards everywhere. Worse, over time the lids wear down and tiny slivers of glass start coming off near the rim — genuinely dangerous if they end up in your food and your stomach. Residue also builds up in the lid corners.

Food-grade plastic containers are the better choice: they don't break if dropped, they're airtight, and they're easy to handle. Since we move these containers around daily, sturdy, good-quality plastic simply makes more sense.

## How do I choose door organizers and cup holders correctly?

Door organizers are some of the best space-savers for a small kitchen — but check the space inside your cabinet before buying. A larger door organizer works where there's enough depth (like the space around a cylinder) for the door to still shut properly. If it doesn't fit and you end up mounting it on the outside of the door, your kitchen won't look as good. Check the size first, then decide.

Cup organizers need the same scrutiny. A tall vertical counter-standing holder advertised for 10–12 cups looks space-saving, but the bottom hooks are basically unusable — any cup hung there touches the surface below because of how the hooks sit. You end up using only the top five or six hooks. Under-shelf hanging organizers are far better: every hook is usable, they save counter space, and nothing is wasted.

## Which grinder should I skip?

The popular multi-purpose grinder looks tempting but is a real waste of money — and often not returnable, so you lose the money entirely. It has no button, so you can't control the speed; it switches straight on when you flip it, forcing you to hold the jars the whole time. It heats up and starts smoking within seconds, which isn't safe. Its dough-kneading attachment is so small it can't make *atta* for even two *rotis*, and overfilling throws everything out the top. If you need a grinder, the plain, simple-looking ones are actually the best.
