You can transform a small, non-modular Indian kitchen with 11 low-cost changes to the sink, trolleys and countertop, most starting at just Rs 100 — no renovation and often no drilling required.

Small kitchens leave us stuck with things we can't easily change, and those little friction points slow down our daily work. These fixes target the busiest, most cluttered zones first.

## How do I fix a kitchen tap that splashes water everywhere?

The sink is the most heavily used area in the kitchen — you wash vegetables, hands, dishes and dump dirty utensils there all day. If the tap splashes or trickles too slowly, the work becomes a hassle and your clothes get soaked.

The fix is a simple tap extension that clips onto the spout and turns the flow into a gentle, shower-like spray — not too forceful, not too slow. It costs only around Rs 100 to Rs 150 and comes with a built-in screw, so you tighten it by hand to fit your tap. No plumber needed. It also filters debris out of the water and stops the outward splash.

Expensive extensions didn't work here; the cheapest one, tried as a last option, turned out best. Its shape fits almost any tap.

## Where should I keep dish brushes and scrubbers?

A floating shelf beside the sink for brushes and scrubbers gets dirty fast, especially if it's white. A silicone draining tray solves this. It comfortably holds three to four scrubbers, the dish-washing brushes, and the soap alongside.

Place it so its pointed end points toward the sink. All the extra water from the brush and soap drains straight down into the sink, so nothing pools and the surrounding area stays clean. The silicone tray is also easy to wash and keep tidy.

## How can I fit more into a small kitchen trolley?

When kitchens are small, we tend to shove things wherever they fit, so utensils get stuck and the trolley won't open. A little thought fixes this. Stack by size:

1. Put the broadest utensils at the very bottom.
2. Nest smaller utensils inside the bigger ones — for example, keep wide bowls inside a large *kadhai*.
3. Place the *kadhai*s and utensils you use daily toward the top for easy reach.
4. Keep big containers you rarely use at the very bottom.
5. Buy utensils that are easily stackable.

This frees up real space, stops things from getting stuck, and lets the trolley open and close smoothly — with no organiser at all. When there isn't space, don't use too many organisers.

## How do I stop cooker rings from getting lost?

Cooker rings get misplaced very quickly and, because similar cookers have identical-looking rings, they get swapped between cookers, which spoils them. After washing and drying a ring, put it straight back into its own cooker's lid. It won't get lost and won't get exchanged.

## Should I label my kitchen containers?

Tea, sugar and salt containers often look identical, and powdered sugar mixes up with salt easily. If your containers didn't come with tea/coffee/sugar printed on them, stick a small tag or write a label with a permanent marker on the lid.

This ends the confusion of opening and checking each one — helpful when someone else, like your husband, also cooks. If a container isn't transparent, labelling it becomes very important.

## How do I use vertical and corner space in a small kitchen?

Even a little corner space can hold a vertical rack or a tall, narrow cabinet, so you don't need side space at all. Open racks give you more room.

- **Under-shelf organiser:** fit one into a shelf with wasted top space to hold cups, freeing the shelf below for boxes.
- **Tall Amazon rack (around Rs 4,500):** its shelves adjust up and down with the included fittings, and it can be wall-mounted if you have no floor space. It's reusable in other rooms too.
- **Old bookshelf:** repurpose an unused one to hold small utensils or countertop items.
- **Corner racks:** specially made to fit awkward corners where nothing else fits, holding several containers.
- **Narrow racks:** slot into the thin, wasted gaps found in every kitchen. Take two, three, four or up to six as needed.

Keeping things openly in narrow gaps means the floor below gets dirty repeatedly and you have to move everything to clean. Narrow racks fix that.

## How can I protect kitchen tiles and walls while cooking?

Cooking throws oil and spice splatter around, and turmeric and *masala* stains often never come off. Place a foldable splatter cover right behind wherever you're cooking on the gas stove. The splatter lands on the cover instead of your tiles or wall.

Wipe it with a wet cloth, fold it, and store it in any small basket. This matters most in a rented kitchen, where you must keep the tiles and wall behind the gas from getting ruined.

## What is a zero-cost way to organize a countertop?

We buy things that then go unused — like a bread container, if you don't eat much bread. Repurpose it: stick double-sided tape on the back, mount it on the tiles, and use it as an organiser. Here it holds a kitchen towel roll — the current one plus a spare — keeping the paper rolls safe and dust-free. It works in the bathroom too, for a toothbrush or toothpaste.
