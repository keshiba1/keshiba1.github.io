You can never fully finish all your housework, but you can absolutely make it easier — by picking a few essential tasks, letting go of perfection, and building small daily habits that keep the house running without draining you.

You don't *just* do housework. You manage an entire home — the kitchen, the cleaning, and taking care of everyone. Here's how to run that whole show with less effort, plus one reliable kitchen trick to start with.

## How do I make caramel without burning it?

Caramel at home either burns too much or never sets — but this method works every time. The key is a high quantity of sugar and a patient, low flame.

1. Add 150 grams of sugar to your pan with just two to three spoons of water.
2. Leave it on medium flame — not too slow, not too fast — and **do not stir at all** in the beginning.
3. The moment you see the colour start to change, begin mixing it a little.
4. Turn the flame down low now, otherwise the caramel will burn. It won't look burnt, but it will taste bitter.
5. Mix gently on low flame until it turns completely liquid. If it looks like it's getting too hot and about to burn, turn off the flame.
6. Once it turns a little transparent and the foam on top disappears and it looks clear, it's ready — turn off the gas completely and don't leave it on the heat.

Pour it straight into whatever you're cooking. I use mine for caramel custard, so I pour it directly into the pan I'll bake the custard in. Caramel works in lots of desserts — you can even drizzle it over pancakes.

## Why does my housework never seem to end?

Every homemaker feels at some point that the housework never ends — and it's true. It's impossible to fully finish, because something new keeps coming up. If you enjoy keeping the house spotless, you'll always find more to do. The goal isn't to finish everything; it's to make everything easier.

## What are the three tasks I should focus on first?

Start by picking three important tasks that absolutely must get done — jobs the house can't really run without. For me, those are cooking, cleaning, and washing dishes or clothes.

Everyone's priorities are different, so choose what matters most for your life. If you're a working homemaker, pick the three or four tasks you need to finish before or after office. And don't aim for every task to be perfect — the more you chase perfection, the longer everything takes. Save perfection for a holiday or a day when you've set aside dedicated time. For daily routine, "good enough" really is good enough. Don't pressure yourself beyond what you can realistically do.

## How do I clean the house faster without mopping everything?

In the mornings I finish my cooking, then move to cleaning — and I vacuum first. Once the floor is vacuumed, the house already looks fairly clean, even without mopping.

I only mop specific spots:

1. **The kitchen sink area** — things spill there while washing dishes or cutting vegetables.
2. **The entrance** — footwear brings in dirt.

Skipping a full-house mop saves a lot of time I can use for something else. A rotating mop makes this easier, and if you have a robot vacuum, even better — it does the floor for you and saves both time and energy.

You also don't need to tidy the whole house every day. Just put things back where they belong as you go, and get the whole family into this habit — it stops clutter building up on the sofa or dining table. I do this while mopping: moving items aside, clearing a table, and putting things back. It takes only a couple of extra minutes if you don't have too much furniture around.

## Should I cook all my meals at once?

Cooking takes a lot of time and usually means multiple trips to the kitchen through the day. If it works for you, do all your cooking together in the morning — breakfast, lunch, dinner, even snacks — instead of coming back in the evening.

Spending fifteen to thirty extra minutes in the morning saves the evening cooking session entirely. It also makes cleanup faster: wash the few dishes that come out of cooking right away, while something is still on the stove, instead of letting them pile up. A full sink keeps nagging at the back of your mind and gets in the way of actually resting.

You don't need to scrub everything daily either — a quick basic wipe-down after cooking is enough. Save deeper scrubbing for once or twice a week, or five minutes before bed so the kitchen looks fresh in the morning.

## Which cloths should I use for kitchen cleaning?

For wiping counters and the stovetop, always use a clean cloth. A dirty cloth just smears grime around and leaves a smell, so you end up wiping the same spot two or three times.

- Wash and dry your cloths properly, and change them every three to four days.
- Use **microfiber cloths** to clean surfaces fastest.
- Use **cotton towels** to absorb water and wipe dishes dry.
- Keep a **separate cloth just for wiping hands**, since that one gets damp fastest.

## What should I do at night to make mornings easier?

At night, nobody has much energy for big chores, but a few small ones make the next morning easier: wash the day's dishes, dry the sink area, clear the counter, and put stray items back in place. You'll wake up to a clean house, which does wonders for your mood, and things move faster from there.

Age-appropriate small chores can also go to other family members to lighten your own load. And keep Sundays light — that's your day to rest and enjoy, not to pile on chores. Instead, spend half an hour planning a task list for the week or two ahead, so you're not stuck deciding what to do first every single day.
