IKEA is worth a dedicated trip for kitchen organisation because it stocks a wider variety than most stores, often cheaper and with good quality — from a ₹100–115 non-leak glass oil dispenser to airtight storage sets that work in both the fridge and microwave.

This is a room-by-room walk through a real IKEA haul in India, with the actual prices spotted on the tags, so you can judge what is worth buying there versus your local shop.

## Which section should I shop first at IKEA?

Start with the kitchen section. It is the biggest area and holds things you may never have seen anywhere else, even online. Tucked right inside it is a small live-plant corner — live plants are usually pricey outside but were reasonably priced here and in great condition. Ceramic pots came in many sizes and designs, with one nice ceramic pot at ₹499 and a sale running for extra discount on top. If you prefer artificial greenery, the range of artificial flowers was larger than most stores carry.

## What crockery and cookware are worth checking at IKEA?

The crockery run had ceramic plates in different colours and shapes, plate-and-bowl sets, and small ceramic bowls (around ₹49–59 each) ideal for soup or cereal. A useful habit was on display here: keep a sheet of paper between stacked glass or ceramic bowls so they do not scratch — IKEA stacks its own bowls with dividers the same way.

Cookware highlights included:

- Bowls made specifically for microwave and oven use — you can cook in them, not just reheat.
- A large glass bowl at ₹499, with other sizes available.
- Silicone bowls and trays for baking and grilling, in round and square shapes.
- Non-stick pans and *kadhai* options, both shallow and deep, plus steel *kadhais* at good prices.
- A three-piece set with a large steel bowl, a saucepan, and a small pan for boiling milk or tea, at roughly ₹4,000–5,000.
- A very large lidded bowl, big enough for family *biryani*, on discount at about ₹2,600.

Separate trivets were available so you never place a hot pan or *kadhai* directly on the counter, alongside hot-plate mats for the dining table.

## What are the most unique kitchen finds at IKEA?

Two items stood out as genuinely hard to find elsewhere: a ceramic oil dispenser that does not leak at all, and a smaller glass version at about ₹100–115 for when you only store a little oil. A bottle-cleaning brush — rarely available online — was very useful for cleaning bottles properly. A glass jar with a tap and a wooden lid worked like a sealed version of the earthen water pots used at home, keeping ants and dust out, and doubling as a party dispenser for juice or cold drinks.

Other novel finds: a clear box holding two smaller boxes inside for the bathroom or separating kitchen items, ice-cube trays as a set of two in cream and green, and a large all-round grater with different grating sizes on each side.

## Which storage containers should I buy for an Indian kitchen?

The storage-container section was the favourite, and for good reason — these are the workhorses of an organised kitchen. Here is how to choose:

1. **Pick airtight, lockable containers first.** A large lidded one was around ₹250 and works in both the fridge and microwave.
2. **Use smaller leak-proof versions as kids' lunch boxes** since they will not break like glass.
3. **Buy sets, not singles** — a five-bowl set with lids, or a six-piece mixed-size set, covers most bedroom and fridge storage at a reasonable price.
4. **Add clips for open packets** to seal biscuits or chips so they stay fresh.
5. **Use clear acrylic organisers** (also on discount) for the fridge, bathroom, or bedroom, with compartment ones for eggs, vegetables, or kids' stationery.

Cream-and-green box sets doubled as *tiffin* boxes and dry-snack storage, and ceramic jars with matching wooden lids kept sugar, salt, tea, and coffee airtight.

## What should I skip buying at IKEA to save money?

Not everything is a bargain. A flour sieve was about ₹300 — cheaper at a local utensil shop. A splatter guard at ₹149 simply matched one already owned. Coffee cups came in lovely colours but felt slightly pricier than similar cups at DMart. A single yellow pot at ₹1,100 was skipped as too much for one item. Glassware in general — glasses, jars, and a ₹799 large water jug — is best kept minimal, since it is harder to manage and breaks easily; reserve glass for decor, like a large jar filled with stones in the living room.

## What steel and serving items are worth a look?

The steel section had strong finds: a strainer usable for rice or for steaming over a large pot, pot-shaped cookers, and regular pressure cookers, all reasonably priced. For hosting, a very large, solidly built serving tray stood out at a good price, and a big rotating tray — a frequent viewer request — could hold plenty of kitchen items. A wood-and-steel combo set worked for both kneading and serving dough, and jute table mats came in several styles.
