You can keep a small Indian kitchen and home clean, organized, and easy to maintain without expensive organizers or hours of labour — the trick is choosing items you can actually wash, using long-handled tools instead of bending, and repurposing containers you already own.

Most of us complain about not having time, but everyone has the same 24 hours. What changes the result is planning the daily cleaning and using smart tricks for the difficult jobs that suddenly land on your head. Here are the kitchen and home cleaning routines I actually use.

## How do I wash medium and small rugs at home without backache?

Heavy carpets are genuinely difficult to wash alone, so I stick to medium and small rugs that I can handle on my terrace. If you don't have a terrace, a balcony with a water arrangement or even the bathroom floor works fine for small rugs.

Here is the exact step-by-step I follow:

1. Spread the rug flat on the floor — terrace, balcony, or bathroom.
2. Use slow-flow water to lightly wet the rug. Don't blast it with pressure — high-pressure water washes the detergent away before it can work.
3. Sprinkle a generous amount of **powder detergent** all over. Powder is more effective on rugs than liquid.
4. Scrub with a **long-handled brush** so you don't have to bend or sit. You can also scrub with your feet, which works surprisingly well on flat rugs.
5. Rinse with slow-flow water. You do not need to flip the rug — the detergent and scrubbing pressure clean the underside automatically.
6. Get someone to help you lift the wet rug. Water doubles its weight, so two people make hanging it to dry much easier.
7. Dry in direct sunlight. On a sunny day, even a white rug is dry and back in the room the same day.

Before you buy any rug or carpet, ask yourself honestly whether you can maintain it. Beautiful things that you can't wash become a long-term burden.

## How do I clean under the bed without moving heavy furniture?

Beds without under-bed storage collect huge amounts of dust underneath, no matter how clean the rest of the room is. A regular *jhadu* is too short, forcing you to circle the bed from all four sides.

My trick: take any long stick — I use the handle from my fan cleaner mop — and tie an old cloth tightly around one end. If the cloth is small, secure it with a rubber band. Keep the cloth **dry** for the first pass so it grabs all the loose dust without needing to be rinsed every few seconds. After the dry sweep, you can follow up with a damp mop if needed. One person, one stick, one cloth — the entire under-bed area is clean in minutes.

## How do I organize the sink cabinet area in a small kitchen?

The sink area sets the tone for the whole kitchen. If it looks cluttered, the whole kitchen looks dirty.

- **Inside the cabinet doors**, fit small hanging organizers to hold your damp *dusting* cloths. Cabinets aren't fully airtight — air circulates through the trolley gaps, so the cloths dry in a few hours without smelling. These organizers are cheap and easy to find.
- **Add a few hooks** for small cleaning tools: a wiper, scrubber, brushes. If adhesive hooks have failed for you in the past, try suction hooks — they hold a little more weight, though even suction hooks can fail if you overload them.
- **Below the sink**, spread newspaper before placing your *tub* and *tokni*. The newspaper catches drips so the cabinet floor stays clean longer.

## How can I organize the fridge without buying any organizers?

The fridge door is where chaos hides. Tiny bottles fall every time you open it, eye drops roll around, kids' chocolates spread everywhere.

Instead of buying fridge organizers, reuse plastic containers whose **lids have broken**. Keep the good containers — they're perfect for grouping small items. I put all my eye drops in one container, chocolates in another. Transparent containers are best so you can see what's inside without opening them. Once everything is grouped, you can slam the fridge door without anything falling, and you actually free up extra shelf space.

## What kitchen design choices save time every single day?

When my kitchen was first built, it had only one plug point — and every appliance had to be carried to that one spot. Now I have plug points on both sides: one near the sink and one near the cooking area where I keep the *mixer grinder*. The lesson: **install plug points in at least two or three places** in any kitchen. You'll move appliances less and save time on every task.

A few other small things that make a daily difference:

- Keep **at least two kitchen towels** — one for wiping hands, one only for wiping washed utensils. This maintains hygiene and means you always have a dry towel ready.
- Hang towels on a **small organizer** so they actually dry. A wet towel left on the counter makes the kitchen look messy and stays damp for hours.
- Place a **small mat** near the sink to catch splashes from utensil washing or rinsing vegetables. Small mats dry faster than large ones, so they're more practical than constantly fetching a full mop.
- Use **slim countertop organizers** for spice bottles and small jars. I bought mine on Amazon years ago and they're still going strong — the links are in the video description.

## How do I keep the rest of the house tidy with zero spending?

For items lying on top of cupboards, almirahs, or shelves, drop them into **baskets** — even the random small baskets already lying around your house. Loose items on a high surface always look messy; the same items inside a basket look tidy. Baskets also make cleaning day faster because you lift one basket instead of fifty small things.
