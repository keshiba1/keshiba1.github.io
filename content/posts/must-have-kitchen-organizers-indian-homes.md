A handful of well-chosen kitchen organizers — a vertical plate stand, airtight *chai-shakkar* containers, command hooks, a rotating tray, an egg holder and a few DIY baskets — will fix most of the daily clutter problems in an Indian kitchen, whether or not it's modular.

Organizing a kitchen is a continuous process. The pieces below are the ones worth investing in because each one solves a specific, recurring problem rather than just adding another empty basket to your shelf.

## How do I organize plates without a modular kitchen?

If you stack plates directly on a shelf, they take up too much space and the bottom plate is always the one you want. A **vertical plate organizer** solves both problems — plates slot in side-by-side, you save counter space, and any plate is grab-and-go.

If you don't want a dedicated plate stand, a sturdy **basket** placed on the shelf works as an alternative. The point is to keep plates upright instead of flat-stacked.

## Which tea and sugar containers are worth buying?

Two options work well, depending on the look you want:

1. **Matching airtight set** — available in ivory and black. The coordinated look is great if you keep containers on an open kitchen rack.
2. **Individual metal tea-sugar containers** — sold separately on Amazon in many colours. They're airtight; the sugar container uses a tight-fit lid rather than a silicone ring, but stays sealed. These add instant colour to the kitchen.

Both are reliable. Pick based on whether you want a uniform set or a pop of colour.

## Why are command hooks so useful in a small Indian kitchen?

Command hooks are the single highest-leverage small organizer for cramped kitchens. They stick almost anywhere — cabinet sides, tile walls, the inside of cabinet doors — and they're strong enough to hold heavy items.

Use them to hang:

- Pans and *kadhai*
- Kitchen tools like *kalchi* and *jhara*
- Kitchen towels
- Lightweight utensils you reach for daily

They free up drawer and counter space without drilling a single hole, which makes them perfect for rentals.

## How do I keep kitchen cabinets organized long-term?

The rule: **don't place items directly inside the cabinet.** Group everything into trays, baskets or rotating organizers first.

Here's the system that keeps cabinets from sliding back into chaos:

1. Sort items by category — baking, snacks, daily *masala* refills, backup jars.
2. Assign each category a tray or basket sized to the cabinet shelf.
3. For deep cabinets, add a **rotating tray** (lazy Susan) so you can reach the items at the back without unloading the front row.
4. When it's time to clean, pull the entire tray out, wipe the shelf, slide the tray back. Two minutes instead of twenty.

A rotating tray in particular is one of those buys you won't regret — it turns a hard-to-reach corner into usable storage.

## What is the best way to store eggs in an Indian kitchen?

Use a dedicated **egg organizer** that holds 24 eggs in fitted slots. Loose eggs in trays or door shelves roll, knock, and crack the moment you shift anything around them. A slotted holder keeps every egg separated and stable.

If your household goes through eggs regularly, this single organizer pays for itself by preventing breakage.

## How can I use the empty space on top of my kitchen cabinets?

That dead space above the cabinets is one of the most under-used storage zones in Indian kitchens. Place a **basket** up there — a DIY one works just as well as a store-bought basket — and use it for extra grocery items you won't need immediately.

Think: backup *atta* packets, refill pouches, festival-only items, surplus jars. Anything you bought ahead but don't reach for daily. This clears your main cabinets for everyday use and turns hidden vertical space into real storage.

## How do I stop glassware from clinking and shaking in the trolley?

When glasses sit loose in a trolley, they shake and clink every time you pull it open — and over time, they chip.

The fix is to use **DIY trays and baskets** to separate glasses by design or set. Each tray holds one set, the tray sits steady inside the trolley, and the glasses no longer collide. You can use sturdy cardboard boxes, plastic baskets, or any flat-bottomed tray you already have.

## How do I stop onions and potatoes from making a mess?

Onions and potatoes shed dry peels through wire baskets, leaving the shelf or trolley below dirty. **Line the basket with aluminium foil** before placing them inside. The foil contains all the loose peels and dust, and you can lift it out and replace it in seconds.

It's a zero-effort fix that keeps the storage area clean without changing the basket.

## What's the bigger principle behind all of this?

Organizing the kitchen isn't a one-time project — it's a habit of matching each problem to the right small tool. Vertical storage for plates. Airtight containers for *chai* and *shakkar*. Hooks for hanging. Trays inside cabinets. Foil under onions. Each fix is small. Together they keep the kitchen working without daily firefighting.
