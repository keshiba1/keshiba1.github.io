A small kitchen with no pantry stays clutter-free when you store extra groceries unopened inside large transparent containers, keep the freezer for spoil-prone items, and never let anything sit loose on the counter.

A messy, cluttered home rarely lets your mind feel calm — it's easy to slip into negative thoughts. The cleaner and more organized your home is, the more sorted your mind stays. When you look at an unorganized area and don't know where to start, the first step is simply storing your belongings properly, whether your home is small or big. Once that's sorted, everything else tends to fall into place.

## How do I decide what to bring into a small home?

Bring in only as much as you actually need and only as much as your home genuinely has space for. We often get attracted to nice-looking things, buy them, then realize there's nowhere to keep them — and end up throwing out things we already had just to make room. Buy something new only if you truly need it. Groceries are the exception: you have to restock them regularly, and if you don't store them well, they scatter across the kitchen and make even a small space feel stuffed.

## How can I store groceries when there's no pantry or extra storage?

My own kitchen has no dedicated pantry and little extra storage, so everything has to be adjusted to stay easy to find and out of sight. Here's the routine I follow:

1. **Lay newspaper on the countertop first.** Refilling containers always causes little spills, so the newspaper catches everything. When you lift it off at the end, the counter underneath is completely clean.
2. **Stand oil packets upright in a magazine holder.** A newspaper file holder keeps them separate and makes pouring easier. Tossed together in one container, they mix or leak and spoil everything nearby. The holder folds flat when not in use.
3. **Keep small spice packets in one big container.** You can't decant every tiny packet into its own jar — there isn't space. Put them in as-is, and before buying more of a spice you can just check the box to see what's run out.
4. **Store extra unopened packets in large transparent containers.** Spare poha, tea leaves, salt, sugar — anything you won't open for a week or ten days — goes in as-is. Transparent boxes let you see the contents without opening each one.

## Which items should go in the freezer?

Some things spoil at room temperature, and the freezer usually has plenty of spare space. Store *sooji*, *besan* and coconut powder in the freezer. Pulses prone to bugs — *moong dal*, chana, chole — keep safe there too, especially extras that take more than a month to finish; unopened in the freezer, they won't get infested at all.

Dry fruit packets work the same way, or transfer dry fruits into a plastic container and freeze them. During the monsoon, moisture causes fungus and softens walnuts, cashews and other nuts, so freezing is far better if you're not using them daily. *Sooji* or idli rava — roasted or raw — keeps for months, easily lasting the whole year, and oats often come in large packs that are ideal for long-term freezer storage.

## How do I store empty parcel boxes without cluttering the kitchen?

The boxes that food deliveries and parcels arrive in are genuinely useful, so throwing them away feels wasteful. Keep one large transparent container and nest the boxes inside each other — smaller inside bigger — with all the loose lids together in one small box. These come in handy for odds and ends in the fridge, or rubber bands and clips in the bedroom. Transparent boxes mean you don't have to open each one to check inside, and nesting keeps them dust-free so you don't need to rewash before use.

In a small kitchen, nothing should sit around loose. Bigger boxes beat lots of small baskets because they hold more and you need fewer of them. Keep a few large containers and store them in overhead cabinets or under-bed storage — out of sight so the kitchen doesn't look cluttered. I keep another box for spare containers and cake molds, since I bake often; keeping silicone molds covered stops them getting dusty and saves the long drying wait when I need them.

## How do I properly clean and dry a narrow oil bottle?

Oil bottles have narrow necks that are hard to wash and harder to dry. Before travelling for four or five days, I wash mine so it's fully dry by the time we're back:

1. Put a little liquid soap inside.
2. Run hot water through the nozzle and pour that same hot water back into the bottle — hot water loosens oil residue that soap alone won't touch. Handle with a cloth, as the bottle gets hot.
3. Rinse thoroughly; you may need two or three soapy rounds.
4. If it's still greasy, add a bit of toothpaste and a few grains of rice with water and shake well — the rice scrubs off stuck-on grease from inside.

An oil bottle takes four to five days to dry, not hours, because nothing fits inside the neck to speed it up. Prop it upside down for about half an hour, supported between two vessels on a towel, then stand it upright to air-dry. Since leaving it open that long risks dust or insects, once it's mostly dry I put it in the fridge to finish drying safely.

## What's the trick to cooking in stainless steel cookware?

Stainless steel is the easiest to use once you know the technique. Instead of adding oil to a cold pan, let the pan heat really well first — hover your hand above it to check — and only then add the oil. Let the oil warm up too before adding onions, ginger-garlic paste, *sabzi* or whatever you're cooking. Heated this way, the pan behaves almost like non-stick, and nothing sticks or burns.

## How do I keep the under-sink cabinet smelling fresh?

The area under the kitchen sink or bathroom washbasin needs regular attention or it starts to smell. Place a small bowl of dried flower petals (potpourri) inside and close the cabinet — the fragrance is strong and lasts a while, and the same batch works for a month or two. Potpourri is easy to find at any shop, at DMart, or online, and the same trick works in any room.
