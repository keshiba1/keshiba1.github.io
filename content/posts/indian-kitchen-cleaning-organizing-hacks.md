Kitchen work is endless, but a handful of tried-and-tested hacks keep your kitchen clean, organized, and far easier to maintain every single day.

These are the cleaning tools, organizers, and small fixes I have used in my own kitchen for a long time before sharing them here. The results are consistently good, so try them and see for yourself.

## How do I keep my sink and countertop clean without harsh chemicals?

For the sink area, skip the random market sprays and homemade liquid mixtures. Many of them contain chemicals that can harm your hands and affect your skin. Instead, mix a little Vim dishwashing liquid with water and use that. If you scrub your sink area thoroughly every day with this, the surface never gets damaged or stained.

White stains are a separate problem. Using too much soap leaves white marks, and they show up clearly on dark countertops like mine — black, brown, or green. The fix is to dilute your soap heavily:

1. Take any good big bottle.
2. Add just a capful of whatever dishwashing liquid you use.
3. Fill the bottle completely with water.
4. Use this diluted solution for daily cleaning.

Because you are using far less concentrated soap, white stains and hard-water marks stop appearing on the countertop, and your hands don't get damaged either.

## Which cleaning tools should I keep near my kitchen sink?

A few well-chosen tools make all the difference:

- **A good scrubber** — it cleans utensils well, whether non-stick or any other material, and works for countertops and sinks too.
- **A small wiper** — when you wash the countertop with a lot of water, a cloth doesn't clear it quickly. A small wiper removes water fast so the surface dries quickly. Small wipers are easily available.
- **A microfiber cloth** — no matter how wet the surface is, one wipe absorbs all the water. It's soft, leaves no scratches, and works on the fridge, TV, and for dusting other rooms too.
- **A long-handled sink scrubber** — this one has a long handle and space to pour liquid inside, so you fill it once and don't keep adding soap. It also cleans the wash basin and bathroom tiles.

## How do I clean burnt pans and cooker handles?

Indian cooking gets utensils very dirty, and sometimes they burn so badly that a normal scrub won't work. Keep a set of small cleaning brushes near the sink. They reach the inside of a cooker, the area near pan handles, and the spots around screws where dirt collects. These brushes come in three types, and they handle old grease, stuck food, and burnt residue easily. For very stubborn dirt or a badly burnt vessel, soak it in hot water first, then scrub with the brushes.

For delicate items — plastic containers and utensils with lots of designs where dirt gets stuck — use an old toothbrush, or buy a slightly firm one and keep it by the sink. It isn't harsh, so it cleans intricate designs without damaging the surface.

## What kitchen organizers actually last for years?

Some organizers fit into any kitchen and don't wear out:

- **A metal cutlery holder** — I've used mine for many years with no scratches and no rust, even though it's metal. It has four compartments, so you can keep forks, knives, bottle openers, and scissors segregated in one small place without taking up much room.
- **A ceramic garlic container** — most of us don't refrigerate garlic, so store it with a little style on the counter instead. This container has an airtight lid but large holes at the bottom. The airflow keeps garlic fresh for a long time, while a tightly sealed container makes it spoil quickly and attract insects.

## How do I stop the bottom rotis from getting wet?

This is a problem every Indian homemaker faces — the bottom rotis turn soggy from steam and get wasted. Here's the fix:

1. Place a hot-pot stand with some height inside your roti container.
2. Lay your usual roti cloth on top of the stand.
3. Stack the hot rotis on the cloth as normal.

Because the stand lifts the cloth off the base, the rotis and cloth never touch the steam that collects at the bottom. No roti gets wet. You can find such stands at any supermarket or utensils shop — just pick one with enough height.

A folding hot-pot stand is also worth keeping. It opens up to hold any hot utensil on the dining table or counter so the surface isn't damaged, then folds flat to tuck inside a cutlery holder or cabinet when not in use.

## What zero-cost hacks make daily kitchen work easier?

Three small fixes using things you already have:

- **Reuse empty spice boxes as organizers.** Don't throw them away. Wrap one in paper to make it look neat, then store scattered small items like Eno and sauce sachets inside. Attach it to the fridge with a binder clip so they're always findable.
- **Protect your hands from wire scrubbers.** Cut the top portion of an empty bottle where the cap sits and stick it onto your scrubber with a glue gun — it holds even after many washes. Open the cap to add detergent inside, and your hand never touches the wire, so no cuts or skin damage.
- **Stop spoons from slipping into containers.** Tie a few rubber bands or hair bands around the top of your spoon, and it won't sink into the batter or slide into the container.
