Treat your kitchen the same way you decorate your living room and bedroom — keep counters empty, hide heavy items inside shelves, and use baskets and reused boxes to organize the rest.

This is a tour of a small Indian kitchen with no utility area, organized almost entirely with items already in the house: old phone boxes, an old fridge rack, empty jam bottles, plastic bottles, and cardboard. Most organizers are basic baskets from Amazon. Nothing is fancy — but everything has a place.

## How is the kitchen entrance organized?

The entrance door carries a DIY hanging made from leftover cardboard and hand-painted at home. Just inside, on the left wall, two small baskets are mounted: one for daily-use garbage bags, and a smaller one above for camphor boxes.

Directly below sits an under-shelf cabinet dedicated to cake-baking — beaters, brushes, scissors, small machines and tools all live inside baskets so nothing gets misplaced. A small paper list is stuck to the cabinet to track items as they run out, so the next grocery run is never guesswork.

## What goes on the hanging baskets and side rack?

Next to the cabinet, hanging baskets on the cabinet side hold small trays in one and a cake turntable in another — these baskets are strong enough to take real weight. Above is a shelf for extras, and beside it more baskets store glass bottles of honey, *ghee* and other less-frequent items. A larger basket holds big trays and chopping boards.

A separate rack stores onions, garlic and potatoes on top, with milk cartons on the lower shelf.

## How do I keep the kitchen counter empty?

The rule is simple: anything that can go on a wall, inside a shelf, or onto a fold-away rack should not live on the counter.

1. Mount the *belan* and *patta* (chakla) on a wall hanger.
2. Hang towels on a 4-towel wall hanger right beside it.
3. Keep only a small daily-spice rack on the counter — *jeera*, *rai*, *hing*, salt, oil, butter.
4. Use a foldable rack for the mixer-grinder and fold it away when not cooking.
5. Keep heavy containers (*atta*, rice, *dal*, *poha*) inside lower shelves, never on top.
6. Store the cooking-gas cylinder behind a hidden door so the floor reads clutter-free.

A small glass tray on the counter catches cooker whistles and tiny items that otherwise vanish.

## How are the upper cabinets and shelves arranged?

Inside the upper cabinet above the daily-spice rack: *dals*, chickpeas, coriander powder and red chilli powder. An old fridge rack is placed at the back of the shelf as a riser — this lifts back-row containers into view and creates usable space underneath. Any old item lying around the house can be repurposed this way.

An under-shelf rack holds daily teacups, with tea, sugar and salt containers below. Painted phone boxes act as risers here too — they take real weight and only needed a coat of white paint to look intentional. None of the containers are fancy; they are simple containers, kept clean, so they never *look* old.

## How is decoration handled without spending money?

Decoration uses things already in the house:

- Empty glass jars, texture-painted, now sit as decor pieces.
- Empty jam bottles, painted and hung, hold fairy lights inside.
- A wooden spoon holder from Amazon is hand-painted on both sides — flip it for a different look.
- Small leftover baskets are hung on a plain window so it stops looking bare.
- Plastic bottles become planters for money plants, placed wherever a small gap appears.
- An artificial money plant tops the cabinets in another plastic-bottle planter.

The tall decorative shelves (too high for daily access) are reserved purely for painted bottles and plants.

## How is the sink area kept clean?

A small basket above the sink holds cleaning brushes — water drains straight back into the sink. A tray sits under the soap dish to catch soap water. The cabinet above the sink stores extras inside small baskets, again so nothing rolls loose.

## How are the trolleys arranged?

The main trolley stacks tea utensils and a casserole on top. Below: large *kadhai*, big vessels and *thali* plates. Glass bottles and small plastic containers hold spice extras. Spoons sit on washable mats for easy cleaning. Daily-use cookers and small vessels for *dal* and rice live on the lowest shelf for quick reach.

A hanging basket inside the trolley holds dishwasher liquid and detergent. Behind a sliding panel, the gas cylinder hides — visually the kitchen reads clutter-free.

The second trolley stores: school tiffin bottles inside a large container (dust-free), the big pressure cooker used occasionally, *tawa*, extra baskets, thermos and extra bottles in another large container, silver foil tiffins, kitchen toppers in a small box, and the heavy *atta*, rice, *dal* and *poha* containers in the lower shelf.

## What goes on the second counter?

The second counter is reserved for baking and cake-decorating work, so it is also kept mostly empty. A foldable rack holds the juicer; a turntable lets you spin and reach items at the back. Coffee mugs sit nearby, with the microwave directly above. A high decorative shelf displays framed photos of cakes baked in this kitchen — a personal touch that costs nothing.

Medicines live in two baskets on a tall shelf. A small hanging basket holds tiny bottles of hajmola and toothpicks. Dry fruit containers are kept low on purpose — so a child can reach them without help.

## What goes in the overhead cabinets?

Overhead cabinets store only rarely-used items: extra plastic containers, spare baskets, occasional appliances. Daily things stay within arm's reach; "someday" things go up high. Under-cabinet LED lighting makes the kitchen glow at night and dresses up the whole space.
