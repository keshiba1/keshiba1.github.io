The fastest way to save time in the kitchen is to keep only what you use daily, group small things together, and give every item a fixed home so you never dig around for it again.

Small everyday problems at home usually come from routines that take longer than they should, or little mistakes that creep in. The kitchen tricks below save time, make daily work easier, and protect your things—and there's a healthy, kid-friendly recipe at the end.

## Why does my most-used kitchen drawer always get cluttered?

The drawers and cabinets you open and close constantly get messy fastest, because you're always digging through them and rarely putting things back properly. When you can't find what you need mid-cooking, you waste time and energy—and items sitting right in front of you stop registering.

The usual culprits are extra add-on gadgets you use only once or twice a year but still hold onto, plus ordinary items you no longer reach for in daily cooking. Once used, they get tossed back wherever there's space, and the drawer turns into a jumble.

## How do I reorganise a messy kitchen drawer step by step?

Whenever a drawer or cabinet starts feeling chaotic, that's the only moment a proper deep clean actually happens—so use it.

1. **Take everything out.** Pulling it all out is the only time the drawer gets a real clean.
2. **Spray and wipe it fully.** Dust builds up in open drawers from every direction.
3. **Line it.** Use a printed sheet of plain packing paper, a mat, or a plastic sheet—anything that won't get soggy. If you only store dry items, the paper liner lasts a good while.
4. **Sort into sections.** Give the largest section to big spoons and spatulas since there are plenty of them. Keep medium spoons and a lemon squeezer together, and keep one spoon just for oil since it gets used a lot.
5. **Arrange cutlery facing opposite directions** so it sits compactly and is easy to grab.
6. **Use small baskets sparingly.** A small basket holds the plastic spoons you use often for salt and sugar, instead of leaving them loose. Cut a little side space for a butter knife you use only occasionally.

Don't overdo baskets and dividers—too many eat up space and leave nowhere for other things. Once you clear out what you don't need, you free up real space and keep only what you genuinely use.

A zero-cost tip here: the small basket can come from your child's outgrown toys. Kids move on to "grown-up" things, so repurpose the basket instead of giving it away.

## How can I use a small kitchen counter without it looking messier?

If your kitchen is small, make good use of the countertop—but don't let loose items spread all over it, or the space will look smaller and messier. Group small things together instead.

- **Cutlery holder:** If you don't want to buy one, reuse an old jar, box, or any container whose lid has gone bad.
- **Rotating (lazy Susan) tray:** Great for salt, sugar, coffee and tea. No tray? An old plate or tray works too—bigger ones store more. This keeps nothing spilling across the counter.
- **Towel stand:** Instead of tossing kitchen towels around, keep a small stand so they hang and dry properly. They dry faster, smell better, and the kitchen stays neat instead of damp towels piling in corners.
- **Small hooks:** If space is tight, hang things up and out of the way so the kitchen visibly tidies itself.

## How do I protect my countertop and dishes from damage?

Everyday habits quietly damage your kitchen surfaces and dishes. A few small changes prevent scratches, cracks and dust.

- **Never place hot pots or heavy vessels directly on the counter**—it slowly scratches and damages the surface. Use a small stand, an old towel, or a mat.
- **Give serving spoons a home.** Laying them straight on a pot looks messy even in a clean kitchen. Keep a small tray, plate or bowl just for them.
- **Protect ceramic plates when stacking.** Direct stacking scratches them and, since they're heavier, can even crack them. Put something soft between each piece—foam sheets cut into squares sized to the plates stop sliding and prevent scratches.
- **Wrap dishes you use occasionally.** Dust gets in even through closed doors. Wrap fancier plates and bowls in cling film before storing in a cabinet or fridge—they stay dust-free and are ready to use without rewashing. No cling film? A polythene bag or cloth shopping bag works, especially in a closed cabinet.

Since deep cleaning everything constantly isn't realistic, these small steps do the protecting for you.

## How can I keep sofa armrests clean without washing the whole cover?

Armrests get dirty fast from handling, and they're hard to wash often and slow to dry. Spread cotton mats or table mats that match your sofa's colour over the armrests instead of resting hands directly on the fabric. You can also cut and hem an old cotton cloth or towel to fit—either way it protects the armrest, looks tidy, and is far easier to wash and dry than the whole cover.

## How do I make a healthy pizza snack for kids at home?

Kids constantly ask for pizza and burgers, but store-bought patties and buns are loaded with preservatives and refined flour. Making something similar at home is much better for the whole family. This pizza-flavoured potato patty is simple, quick, and anyone can make it.

1. Boil and mash potatoes, then mix in garlic-bread or pizza seasoning, salt, and a bit of black pepper into a soft dough. Adjust salt and seasoning to your kids' taste.
2. Divide into two portions and shape each into a flat, crack-free patty, pressed as flat and even as possible.
3. Cook in butter, not oil—butter gives better texture and flavour. Keep the flame low, brown one side lightly, then flip and let the other side cook through slowly so the inside cooks evenly without burning.
4. Once both sides are lightly browned, spread ketchup (or pizza sauce or mayonnaise), add cheese slices, a few olives, and a sprinkle of pizza seasoning.
5. Cover and let it sit so the cheese melts.

That's a healthy snack with no refined flour and no preservatives, made entirely from real ingredients. Cheese slices work, but pizza-blend cheese makes it even better—use whatever you have.
