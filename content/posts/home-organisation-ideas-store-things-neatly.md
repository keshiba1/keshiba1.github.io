A well-organised, clean home makes every task easier, and most of it comes down to giving small things like wires, clothes, and jewellery a fixed, tidy place.

## How do I organise messy wires so the floor stays clean?

Wires spread across a study table, work desk, or floor make mopping miserable — you end up lifting and shifting them ten times over, and a robot vacuum gets tangled in them. The fix uses things you likely already own.

1. Place your extension board inside a jute basket and fold the dangling wires in with it. To use any plug, part the jute strands in the middle and pull the cord out through the gap so the top stays neat.
2. Set a spare Wi-Fi box underneath and route more loose wires through the slots on its sides.
3. Fix a small hook on the wall for appliance cords — a paper shredder cord, for example — and hang them so nothing rests on the floor.

A jute basket looks attractive, holds plenty, and if you have an open area, a dedicated Wi-Fi jute basket works well too. With this small arrangement the whole area looks far tidier, and you can mop or run a robot vacuum without stopping to move wires.

## How do I keep my bed tidy and dust-free?

Once your bed is made and blankets are folded, spread a simple bedsheet over the top as a cover — an old bedsheet works fine if you don't have a proper bed cover. You can tuck the pillows inside, or if the bed looks better without that, just cover the blankets. Fold it neatly over the top the way hotel beds are finished. The bed looks pretty and no dust settles inside.

## Can I dry a lot of clothes in a small space?

We can't reduce how many clothes pile up daily, and drying space is always short — especially in the rainy season, when clothes hung close together won't dry for two days and start to smell. Damp, smelly clothes can't be worn and have to be washed all over again.

Here's a hack that costs nothing:

1. Take a hanger and gather *fabric*-type rubber bands — the cloth hair ties most homes have lying around. Avoid thin elastic bands; they can't take the weight and will snap.
2. Attach the rubber bands along the hanger, leaving space between each one.
3. Loop one garment through each band so the clothes hang with gaps between them.

The spacing lets air flow between garments so they dry quickly, indoors or out, in any season. Make loops on three or four hangers and you can dry many clothes together in very little space.

## How often should I clean behind kitchen appliances?

Pull out your kitchen appliances and look — a lot of dust settles underneath and behind them, and it's slow to clean if you leave it too long. Check every 10 to 15 days and wipe behind and beneath them with a wet cloth. Cleaning little and often keeps it from ever getting badly dirty.

For the tops, lay a small mat, an old cotton cloth, or newspaper over the appliance. Cooking steam rises continuously and makes dust stick hard to the surface. A removable cover is far easier to wash or swap than scrubbing the appliance itself.

## Where should I store jewellery so I actually wear it?

When jewellery sits hidden inside boxes, searching for a piece before a function is such a hassle that you often skip wearing it. Storing it in view solves that.

Fix a hanging organiser to the inside of your wardrobe door. It's sized to fit inside any cabinet or wardrobe door, and its big advantage is stability — load all the heavy jewellery on one side and light pieces on the other and it still won't tilt or swing, unlike ordinary hanging organisers that lean the moment one side is heavier. It's strong enough for kitchen items too, and works in the bedroom or bathroom for scrubbers and small towels. Your jewellery stays organised and visible, the wardrobe looks neat, and getting ready is quick. This organiser cost just ₹199.

## How do I keep a very large suitcase dust-free?

Small suitcases and handbags can be nested inside a bigger suitcase to stay dust-free — but the largest suitcase on top is the problem, since bags rarely come in that size. Instead of draping an old bedsheet over it, use a large storage bag big enough for the biggest suitcase.

1. Pull the bag over the suitcase from the top so it's fully covered — get someone to help so it packs straight.
2. Push out all the trapped air.
3. Tie a tight knot at the top (or the bottom, whichever is easier).

No dust gets in, and even your biggest suitcases stay clean and protected in storage.

Every one of these ideas uses things already at home or a very low-cost buy — small arrangements that make the big, tiring tasks of a home much easier.
