The biggest kitchen savings come not from cutting essentials but from reusing free items, diluting concentrated cleaners, freezing what spoils, and cutting gas and electricity waste — small habits that add up to real money by year's end.

## How can I save money using free items that come with my shopping?

Every homemaker already receives useful things for free without noticing. Medicine boxes come with little **silica gel** pouches that fight moisture and smell. In the rainy season, place one or two in any corner of a wardrobe — especially wooden ones that hold dampness and odour — and the smell disappears. Leave a little empty space in the wardrobe so air circulates and any smell can escape. Drop the same pouches into food storage boxes so no moisture builds up inside.

Old cups and candles have a second life too. Melt the wax from a candle you never use and pour it into an old cup sitting unused in your crockery shelf. Once it hardens, press an *agarbatti* (incense stick) into it. The holes that form let you slot a fresh stick into the same spot, the ash falls inside the cup instead of dirtying the surface, and two unused items become one useful holder.

## What kitchen items should I stop buying to save money?

Some purchases quietly drain your grocery budget. Paper towel rolls are a common one — they are costly, one roll never lasts a month, and the mess in Indian kitchens is more than thin paper sheets can handle. Cut them from your monthly spend and use one of these instead:

1. Cut old cotton clothes into small cleaning pieces.
2. Use old towels the same way.
3. Invest once in a microfiber cloth that lasts years — you'll tire of using it before it wears out.

The principle: pay once for things that last two or three years rather than repeatedly for disposables.

## How do I store expensive crockery without cling film?

An earlier hack of wrapping cabinet crockery in cling film worked, but cling film is expensive and can't be rewrapped every time. A free alternative: old *tiffin* bags. Kids' school or husband's office tiffin bags, once the handles loosen, are usually thrown away — but they're made of thick material with plenty of room inside.

Use them to store the good crockery you only bring out for parties. Put quarter plates and bowls in a smaller bag, dinner plates in a bigger one, and tape each shut from the side with cellotape so it won't open from the top. No dust settles, no scratches form, and you skip the wash-before-use step. If you don't have tiffin bags, double up two thick shopping bags and store the crockery the same way.

## How can I organise clutter without buying an organizer?

Small items left out on open shelves make a home look untidy. Instead of buying an organizer, reuse cardboard. Gift hampers often come with base trays you can keep, or take any carton, wrap it neatly with paper, and store the loose items inside. It looks tidy and costs nothing.

## Can I dilute cleaning liquids without losing their effectiveness?

Yes. Cleaning sprays and liquids like Colin, Lizol, Dettol and fabric conditioner are strong and full of chemicals, so mixing water in doesn't reduce how well they clean — diluted use is often better. Keep two or three empty bottles and divide one bottle of product across them. One bottle then lasts three to four months. The same works for kitchen dishwashing liquid. This isn't stinginess; it's your home and your savings, and no one else's business.

## How do I save gas, water and time while cooking?

Fewer utensils mean less gas, less water and less dishwashing liquid. A few habits make the difference:

1. Use bowls that work for both serving and reheating, so fewer vessels come out.
2. Store fridge leftovers in microwave-safe containers you can reheat straight from the fridge.
3. Cook with a cooker with separator containers — dal and rice together in one cooker saves gas and time.

These separator containers are easy to find online or at any utensil shop, and you can use as many tiers as you like.

## Why should I keep spices and grains in the freezer?

At room temperature, extra spice packets attract moisture and can spoil even while sealed and unopened. The freezer prevents this for months with no preservatives added. Store extra chana, rajma and chole there, along with boondi, sooji and besan. Dry red *mirchi* (chillies) spoil and get infested if left out, so freeze those too. Take out only the daily quantity and keep the rest frozen.

## How do I cut my electricity bill in the kitchen?

The fridge is where careless habits cost the most. When cleaning it, the door has to stay open — so switch it off at the main switch first, then clean the trays as long as you need. Leaving it open and powered on consumes a lot of energy and raises the bill noticeably. During everyday use, open the fridge, take out what you need quickly, and shut it fast.

## How do I reduce food wastage with one simple trick?

Keep one fixed-size bowl or cup inside your grain container and always measure with the same one. A consistent measure tells you exactly how much rice or dal you use daily and how many meals that makes, so you cook the right amount and waste less.
