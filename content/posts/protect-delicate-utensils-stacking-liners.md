Stacking delicate utensils in a tight kitchen doesn't have to mean scratches or cracks — a thin spacer between each piece protects them completely, even when space forces you to pile everything up.

When storage space runs out, most of us stack utensils on top of each other because there's nowhere else to put them. Steel handles this fine. Glass and ceramic do not. This post focuses on one specific problem in a crammed Indian kitchen: how to store your delicate and expensive utensils in almost no space without damaging them — plus a few small habits that keep the whole kitchen easier to manage.

## Why does direct stacking ruin glass and ceramic utensils?

Glass, ceramic and similar materials are delicate. When you stack them directly, the touching surfaces rub and press under the weight of the pieces above. That is where scratches appear, and where edges and rims chip or crack. These utensils are often the costly ones in the kitchen, so a small storage habit protects real money. The fix is simple: never let two delicate surfaces sit against each other bare.

## How do I stack bowls and glasses so they don't get scratched?

Keep a soft spacer between every piece. The easiest one is a cupcake paper — you get a large number in a single packet, so they cost almost nothing per use.

Here is the method:

1. Take a cupcake paper and flatten it out a little.
2. Place it in the centre of the bowl, or along the side where the next bowl will rest.
3. Set the next bowl on top so the paper sits between the two surfaces.
4. Repeat for each bowl in the stack.
5. If your bowls are larger than the paper, fold two cupcake papers together and arrange them so the contact area is fully covered.

The same idea works for glasses and cups. Place a cupcake paper inside or between each glass before stacking, and tuck one between ceramic cups too. Wherever two pieces would otherwise touch, the paper takes the contact — so no scratches, no chipped edges, no fear of cracking.

If you don't have cupcake papers, plain paper, old notebook pages or napkins do the same job. Keep a sheet between each utensil and you can store everything in very little space without harm.

## What can I line my drawers and trolleys with for free?

Use an old mat you already own. Instead of placing utensils directly on the cabinet, trolley or drawer surface, lay down a liner first.

- An old floor mat that has stopped looking good on the floor can be cut to size and spread inside a trolley.
- An unused yoga mat works even better — cut it into small pieces, wash it well, and place it as a liner. It doesn't break easily and stays soft, so delicate utensils resting on it won't get scratched.

A liner does several jobs at once. Any dust rising from below collects on the mat instead of on your utensils. Small items are less likely to slip down through gaps. Delicate pieces stay scratch-free because the surface is soft. And your cabinets, drawers and trolleys simply look nicer. Best of all, these mats are easy to shake out or wash whenever they get dusty.

## How do I keep everyday-use items visible in a small kitchen?

Hang them where you can see them. A small hanging organizer — the kind available on Meesho for around ₹99–100, and also in local shops or on Amazon — comes with adhesive tape and hooks so you can mount it on a tiled wall. Because it is small, it barely uses any space, yet it holds several daily-use items right in front of you so nothing goes missing behind cabinet doors.

For tiny loose pieces that get lost easily — cooker whistles are the classic example — a small basket is ideal. A basic basket bought for as little as ₹10 from a local market can last for years without cracking. Hang it in a corner so its contents stay visible from the front, and clean it before use. Lose a cooker whistle and the whole cooker becomes useless, so a cheap dedicated basket is worth having.

## How do I refill jars without making a mess?

Refilling powders one spoon at a time is slow and still spills, which then adds to your cleaning. Instead, make a cone from an old piece of paper and use it as a spout: set the cone into the jar's mouth and pour the masala, powder, sugar or salt straight through. It refills quickly, stays clean and saves time — especially with small bottles where spills are worst. A funnel works too, but choose a slightly larger one; a tiny funnel makes liquids like oil trickle through and wastes your time.

## How do I stop garbage going down the sink drain?

Use a mesh net over the sink. A large mesh or net can be cut to your sink's size and spread across it, or placed inside or under the sink area. Water passes straight through while any food waste or garbage is caught on top instead of slipping down the drain. Cut it to whatever size suits your sink and set it the way you prefer.

## What's the one habit that keeps all of this working?

Clean regularly. When you tidy and wipe as you go, you rarely need a big deep-clean session. Combined with protected stacks, free liners and visible daily-use storage, small consistent effort keeps even a cramped kitchen organized and your costly utensils safe.
