The fastest way to stop wasting time on housework is to fix why a spot is hard to clean, not to keep re-cleaning it. After a home is organized, we still end up putting the same items back again and again, and some things are placed so awkwardly that removing them at cleaning time is itself a big task — so that corner often gets skipped entirely. This post takes a preventive angle: four tiny tools that remove the repeated effort at its source, plus the budget organizer picks worth buying if you're setting up a home.

## Why does the same corner keep getting skipped during cleaning?

Because the effort to clear it outweighs the two minutes of cleaning. When a tray is stuck to a table cover, or a net has a hole you keep meaning to fix, the friction is in the *setup*, not the wiping. Solve the setup once and the maintenance disappears. Each fix below targets a recurring, low-drama problem most of us live with for months without addressing.

## How do I clean graters and sharp utensils that a scrubber can't reach?

Graters are in near-constant use and are among the hardest utensils to wash, because food stays stuck inside the grooves and a normal scrubber can't reach in — worse, the sharp edges cut the scrubber.

1. Keep a brand-new toothbrush near your kitchen sink, reserved only for utensils.
2. Brush the inside grooves of the grater under running water; trapped food lifts out in seconds.
3. Use the same brush for any small, ridged, hard-to-reach utensil.

This is a fresh brush kept specifically for the job, not a used one. It's the single easiest change that makes washing kitchen utensils less of a chore.

## How do I keep dust off clothes I only wear occasionally?

Wooden wardrobes look lovely but have small gaps that let dust creep in — unlike old metal almirahs, which have no gaps at all. The clothes that suffer most are the ones we don't check daily: an occasional saree, suit, coat or bag. By the time you finally look, dust has already settled.

The fix is a fabric clothes cover. You hang a whole bunch of clothes together and slip the cover over them, sealing out dust completely. It holds plenty, and if your wardrobe has vertical room you can stack one hanger below another — like the way bangle rings attach in a line — to fit even more. It's good quality and not costly. Anything can go inside: sarees, suits, coats, bags.

## What stops a tray from sticking to the plastic cover on my table?

Many of us lay a transparent plastic cover on the dining table to protect the tablecloth. The problem: a tray loaded with items sticks to that cover, and when you're cleaning in a hurry, pulling the tray drags the plastic cover along with it.

Use small furniture pads — the kind normally stuck under furniture legs. Stick them under your tray, and it glides instead of clinging. This works under any tray or decorative piece, on a dining table, coffee table or TV table, and it protects the surface underneath from damage too. Shifting things during cleaning becomes effortless.

## How do I seal a small hole in a window mosquito net?

Mosquito netting on windows and doors keeps out mosquitoes, flies and even lizards — until a small gap or tear appears and tiny insects start slipping through. Net stickers solve this. They come in tape form with adhesive on one side: peel the backing paper off and press the sticker over the hole. They work on fabric or metal, don't look too bad, and close the small gaps that insects exploit.

## Are big storage containers the smartest buy for a small home?

Quite possibly. IKEA's container section had sizes I had never seen in any plastic shop — from very small up to a 130-litre and a roughly 65-litre container. A large one around 999 is reasonable value against the 499 to 699 I'd paid before for smaller ones, and these were very strong.

In a small home, small items stay scattered because there isn't enough space. Take one big container, gather everything scattered into it, and the room instantly looks organized — with the bonus that stored things don't get spoiled. A container this big can even reduce how much wardrobe space you need.
